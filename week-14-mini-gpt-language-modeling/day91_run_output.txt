2. COUNTING MODELS -- add-k (Day 90) against Kneser-Ney
======================================================================
Both count what followed each context in the training text. Add-k adds k to every count. Kneser-Ney subtracts a discount D from every seen count, gives the saved probability to the next shorter context, and below the top order counts how many DIFFERENT characters came before a string instead of how often it occurred. D is chosen on the last 10% of the training text (order 5), k as on Day 90. Held-out bits per character; every model scores the same characters (from the 17th on), each with its real preceding context.

discount chosen on held-back training text: D = 0.9 (tried 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99)
